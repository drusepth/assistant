**Mobile nav within thumb reach: a bottom toggle and an edge swipe**: the drawer's only opener was the hamburger in the sticky header, which is where an eye looks for a menu and also the one corner of a 900-point phone a thumb can't reach without regripping. The instinct is to add a swipe, but a swipe can't carry it: both mobile browsers claim the left-edge drag for their own back gesture and never forward those touches to the page (only an installed `display: standalone` PWA gets the edge to itself), and an invisible gesture is no affordance for a first-time user or a screen reader. So the fix is a second *button* — a floating one at the bottom-left of the viewport, the edge the drawer arrives from, leaving the bottom-right slot for a primary action, offset by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` so it clears the home indicator, and at `z-20` under the backdrop and drawer so an open drawer just covers it instead of needing a second state to keep in sync. The swipe ships too, as the shortcut it can honestly be: drag right from a 48px left band to open, drag the open drawer left to close, threshold-based rather than finger-following so a cancelled touch can't strand it half-open, with a slope test (a vertical scroll is not a drawer gesture), a 10px deadzone (at dx = 0 every gesture looks vertical), an ancestor check that yields to anything horizontally scrollable, and a media query making it inert at `lg` where the rail is furniture. Verified by driving real `Touch`/`TouchEvent` sequences through Chromium at Pixel-7 size: the button opens, the edge drag opens, the drawer drag closes, and a mid-page drag, a vertical scroll in the band, a 35px twitch, and any swipe at desktop width all correctly do nothing. `docs/DESIGN.md` gains the general rule under Narrow screens — a control every page needs belongs within thumb reach, and gestures may shortcut it but never replace it — and `test/integration/mobile_layout_test.rb` pins the bottom-anchored opener and its safe-area offset.
