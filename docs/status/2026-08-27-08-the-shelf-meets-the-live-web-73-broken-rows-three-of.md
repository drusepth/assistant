**The shelf meets the live web: 73 broken rows, three of them our bug**: the starter-feeds job ran against the open web for the first time and came back **217 OK · 36 moved · 73 broken** out of 326. Most of that is what a hand-assembled list of strangers' URLs always is — but three of the failure *shapes* were the app's, not the publishers', and each one was costing working feeds for real users, not just for CI.

**`Web::Fetch` sent a bare-token User-Agent**, and ~30 ordinary publisher feeds answered 403 to it: Mr. Money Mustache, Stronger by Science, Shut Up & Sit Down, Harvard Health, Lawfare, KevinMD. Naive WAF rules (Wordfence, Sucuri, Cloudflare's bot rules) refuse anything that doesn't parse as `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; …)` — the shape Googlebot and every feed reader since Bloglines has used. It is now that shape, still naming the app and linking to its source, and it changes every fetch the app makes rather than only the shelf's. **The 2MB page ceiling is wrong for a feed**: a full-content Atom file clears it routinely, and three legitimate feeds (danluu.com, Podnews, Hagerty) were being refused outright — which is not an error anybody sees, it's a subscription that silently never updates. `max_bytes` is per call now, the way the timeouts already were, and the feed paths pass 8MB. **A response with no `Content-Type` was refused rather than sniffed**, which cost Bandcamp Daily; a declared type still wins, so the fallback can't be used to talk the fetcher into reading a binary as text. All three have regression tests, and all three would have gone on quietly losing feeds indefinitely without a job that actually fetches.

The checker learned two things from its own output. **NO ITEMS is now a failure state** — CFPB's blog feed and AKC's parsed perfectly and carried zero entries, which puts an empty river on a brand-new hobby card, broken in the way that matters even though every byte is valid. And **failures print first**, because a run with seventy things to fix should not open with two hundred lines of OK.

In the list itself: 33 URLs corrected where CI reported a real relocation — Quanta off its API host, It's FOSS to `feed.itsfoss.com`, Lichess to its `@/Lichess` path, Powder and Surfer to their opaque `.rss/feed/<uuid>` addresses, World of Tea and Fringe Association to the sites their authors renamed them to. Geo and session redirects (`?scope=anon`, the Guardian's `/us/`, ANN's `?ann-edition=us`) were deliberately **not** written down: following those pins every self-hoster to one edition. ESPN's four rows are gone — it has dropped RSS entirely, and every one of them answered with a page. Three Future plc sites (Cycling Weekly, Guitar World, Guitar Player) had all made the same wrong guess and moved to the `/feeds/all` their platform actually serves. Six rows swapped for live alternatives, five dropped for dead hosts or broken TLS. 321 feeds across 126 topics, every topic still carrying at least two.

**Round two — 264 OK · 15 moved · 42 broken** — forced the distinction that decides whether this job can ever be green: **refused is not the same as wrong.** Two thirds of what was left were 403s, 5xx and timeouts, which is a publisher declining to serve robots from a datacenter; Cloudflare and Wordfence do it by policy and the same URL answers a home connection fine. Failing on those would mean the shelf could only ever hold feeds whose publishers tolerate GitHub's IP ranges — a worse list than the one we want, and for a reason that has nothing to do with the reader. So REFUSED prints, counts, and doesn't fail. A 404, a host that doesn't resolve, a certificate that doesn't verify, a body that isn't a feed, or a feed with nothing in it does fail, because those a human has to fix. And a REFUSED *rate* over 20% fails as well, so the check can't quietly become "green because nothing answered" — which is precisely what it does say when run from this sandbox, where the egress proxy 403s all 315 rows and the job answers "that is a fetcher problem, not 315 publishers having a bad day."

Round two's real fixes: Future plc turned out to serve `/feeds.xml`, not the `/feeds/all` three rows had guessed; nine addresses that answered with a page, a 404, a bad certificate or an empty feed were replaced (Live Science for HeritageDaily, Barbecue Bible for AmazingRibs, EDN for EEVblog, Permanent Style for Die Workwear!, New Elementary for Brickset, Import AI for a BAIR that timed out twice); six were dropped where the topic kept two without them. 315 feeds, 126 topics. The workflow also gained a concurrency group — GitHub matches a `paths` filter against the whole PR diff rather than the last commit, so a docs commit on this branch had started a second full sweep of three hundred strangers' servers while the first was still running.

The lesson worth keeping: **a list of other people's URLs cannot be reviewed, only measured.** This was assembled carefully and 22% of it was wrong on the first contact with the web — and the most valuable failures weren't the dead links at all, they were the three bugs in our own fetcher that a hundred hand-checked rows would never have surfaced.
