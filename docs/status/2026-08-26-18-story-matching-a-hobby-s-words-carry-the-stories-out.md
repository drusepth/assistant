**Story matching: a hobby's words carry the stories out of a broad feed (D-story-matching)**: all four waves of `docs/STORY_ROUTING_PLAN.md`, built. D-hobby-feeds files feeds and never stories, which stranded every birding story inside a general news feed — unreachable from Birding, and unfixable by filing that feed there, which would drown the hobby in the other 95%. The way out spends model calls on the **rule** instead of on the classified: a `Watchlist` is a per-user list of words that mean one hobby (plus negatives that don't), and `Feeds::Match` applies it at ingest in plain Ruby — a term in the title, or two distinct terms anywhere, and no negative. One sentence, because it has to be explainable on the page where it's edited. Matching costs no model call, no network and no index, forever; authoring costs one call per hobby against the ~3,000 stories a month per-story classification would have charged for. **W1 has no AI in it at all** — hand-typed words, offline matching, the editor, the tell, the verb — which is how the no-model instance gets the whole feature rather than a degraded one. Exceptions live in their own `feed_item_matches` table with a `source`, so `FeedItem` stays not-`Sectionable`, `section_taggings` keeps meaning "the user's filing of primitives", and a rule rebuild can never overwrite a human's row or forget a rejection. D-feed-reader's invariant is amended in one direction only: an item's tags are its feed's tags, **plus any exception recorded on the item**. The river gains one OR *inside* its existing `user.feeds` scope, so the reader's single authorization line doesn't move and no grant can widen what a match shows; the catch-all keeps its copy, because the feed is still unfiled and nothing should vanish from where you last saw it. W2 authors the words from the hobby's name, its feeds and the titles you actually read, add-only (a word you typed survives it, a word you threw out is never proposed again) — and every candidate is tested against your last 500 stories first, with anything matching >20% dropped and explained in the editor. W3's "Not this" tombstones the row and counts against the terms that fired; a term rejected three times and rejected more often than read comes off the list. W4 pauses a rule for a feed it floods (>40% of a week), names the words, drains the unread matches it caused and keeps the read ones. Two design changes the building forced, both about the guard being something a person can live with: a correction **removes the over-broad term** rather than adding the fired one to the negatives (negating "warbler" would kill every real warbler story), and a **resume outranks the guard** — without it, "Watch it again" started a fight the app won every night. One bug worth remembering: `update!` writes only *changed* attributes, so a stale copy of a row silently declines to write rather than overwriting — `suspend!`/`resume!` reload first.
