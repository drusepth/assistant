**One feed row open at a time**: the river's expand-in-place (D-reader-density) had no accordion, so on a phone every row you tapped stayed open behind you — an expanded panel is blurb plus four 44px verbs, roughly a third of a 390×844 screen, and two of them meant the item you actually tapped got pushed below the fold by the one you'd finished with. Opening a row now closes whichever was open. Two details do the work. **The row you tapped doesn't move**: a panel closing *above* it takes its height out of the page and everything below slides up, which on a phone lands you reading something you didn't pick — so `collapseOthers` measures the opening row's `top` either side of the collapse and hands the difference back with `scrollBy`, closing the others with `transition: none` because the compensation is one jump and a 200ms shrink would drift out from under it. **The row that arrives late loses**: opening marks read, which re-renders that row *open* (`from=expand`), and that render can land after the reader has moved on — tap a second row, or tap the same one shut, while the request is in flight and the first would pop back open behind them. A module-level `openRowId` records which row is open (`null` when none is), and a row whose `connect` finds itself `is-open` while that says otherwise removes the class before it is painted; Stimulus connects off a MutationObserver microtask, so there's no flash. It's global rather than gated on a media query — a behaviour that differs by viewport is a second mode nobody can predict, and two open panels are redundant on a laptop too, where the collapsed row already carries the blurb. Row-controller-local rather than a `river` listener on purpose: the same partial renders inside the Hobbies lens card, which has no river controller, and the accordion has to hold there too. `docs/READER.md` updated; the pre-existing time-of-day failure in `test_the_river_is_grouped_under_a_day_spine` predates the branch.
