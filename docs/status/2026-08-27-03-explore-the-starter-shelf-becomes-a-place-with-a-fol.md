**Explore: the starter shelf becomes a place, with a Follow button on every row**: widening the shelf to ~150 sources across 48 topics exposed the thing that had been fine at fourteen rows — it was only ever reachable from an empty state. The reader's empty state, the feed list's empty state, the Hobbies panel with no feeds: three surfaces, all gated on having nothing to read, so following one feed hid the entire library forever. `/feeds/explore` (**D-feed-explore**) is the same list as a destination, linked from the reader, the feed list and the shelf itself. Same promise, unchanged: the static file in the repo, identical for every install, ordered by the file, nothing personalized, nothing scored, nothing fetched to build the page, nothing followed until a button is pressed — READER.md's first promise was never about *when* you could reach the shelf. What changed shape is the verb. A tick-and-submit form is right when the answer is "give me a starter set"; it is wrong when the answer is "I got a dog last week", so Explore's verb is one click per row, with a "Follow all N" per topic for the case the form used to serve — both through the same `Feeds::Starters.import`, so no new fetch posture and no new parse path. Following still creates the topic's hobby lens, for the same reason ticking a group did: a row picked out of *Birding* is somebody saying they're interested in birding, and the flash says so ("1 feed added, filed under Birding"). Rows already followed stay on the shelf marked "Following" rather than vanishing — `Feeds::Starters.unfollowed_for` existed and would have been less code, but a topic that quietly loses rows reads as a list that changed its mind. The search is the Address Book's shape (D-address-book-directory): a real GET form so a query deep-links and works with JS off, `shelf_controller.js` narrowing on the keystroke over it, and both matching `Entry#haystack` so the server and the browser can't disagree about what a query finds. Two smaller things fell out: `Feeds::Opml.entries` now carries `htmlUrl` through to `Starters::Entry#site`, so a row's name links at the publisher's page instead of at raw XML (with the reader's `OUTBOUND` posture — new tab, no referrer), and `Entry#host` replaced the inline `URI.parse(…) rescue nil` the shelf partial had been carrying. Checked in a browser end to end: one click follows exactly that source, the row flips to Following, the topic's bulk verb drops to "Follow all 2", and the search survives the round trip.
