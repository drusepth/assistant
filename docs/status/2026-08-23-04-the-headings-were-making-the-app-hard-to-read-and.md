**The headings were making the app hard to read, and the cause was faux-bold (`docs/DESIGN.md` § Type)**: Caprasimo is a one-weight family, so every `font-bold`/`font-semibold` sitting on a heading never loaded a bolder cut — the browser *synthesized* one by smearing the outlines of a face that is already very heavy, which is what turned page titles into blobs. The `@layer base` rule compounded it by giving the display face to all of `<h1>`–`<h4>`, so it also landed at `text-sm` card titles, and `.btn` put it inside every button, where a 14px label had no room for it. Three fixes, all at the source: headings now default to **Figtree at 600** (`-0.01em`, `1.3` leading) and buttons went with them, so the interface is lettered in the face designed for interface text; the display voice became an opt-in **`.display` atom** that pins `font-weight: 400`, sets `letter-spacing: 0` (negative tracking is a correction for airy grotesques — on closed counters it welds them shut) and 1.18 leading; and `font-synthesis-weight: none` on that atom means a stray `font-bold` added later renders honestly instead of smearing. Caprasimo now appears only where it has room to be characterful rather than merely heavy — the `Ui::PageHeader` title, the household hero, the sidebar wordmark, the recipe monogram, the public share pages — which keeps the brand voice while giving back the legibility. Verified by rendering real pages through the integration harness and screenshotting them against the compiled stylesheet at phone width, before and after.
