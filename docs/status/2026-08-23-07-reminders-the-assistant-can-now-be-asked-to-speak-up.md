**Reminders: the assistant can now be asked to speak up later**: the phone channels that landed an hour ago could alert, but nothing in the app could be told *when* — `recurring.yml` is cron fan-out and no item carried a send time, so "remind me at 4pm" had nowhere to live. It has one now (D-reminders), and the shape came out of two decisions worth keeping. A reminder owns no content of its own, so it is **not a primitive**: `reminders` is a polymorphic row over an Event or a Task, or over **nothing at all** — the standalone "call the pharmacy at 4pm" is the case the feature exists for, and a `reminder_at` column bolted onto Event could never have served it. And `deliver_at` is **always absolute**, even for a reminder set as "an hour before", which keeps the dispatcher a single indexed query; the lead time is re-applied by a `Remindable` hook whenever the item moves, because "an hour before" becomes a lie the moment an appointment is rescheduled, and one reminder firing at the old time is enough to stop someone trusting the feature ever again. Clear a task's due date and its relative reminders retire rather than firing at a moment that no longer exists. Delivery polls every five minutes instead of enqueueing a job at `deliver_at` — a queued job can't cleanly be un-queued when the user edits or deletes the thing, whereas a poll reads the current truth each tick — and it claims each row with an atomic conditional update before sending, because the timer can otherwise enqueue the same reminder twice and a double send on the SMS channels costs real money. Anything more than a day late is retired unsent: a ping about a meeting that already happened reads as the app being broken, not as diligence. Times are composed and rendered in the household's zone explicitly rather than in `Time.zone`, which is only the household's zone inside a request — the dispatcher is a job, and that gap is exactly the kind of thing that makes a 4pm reminder arrive at 9am. The rule worth stating loudest: a reminder **ignores the agent dials**. D22's Off/Quiet/Active says how chatty the agent may be, and a person who set an alarm has already answered that question — same stance as the email plan's user-armed boomerang, no model in the path and no dial in the way. The per-channel daily cap still applies, because that one is the user's own stated budget. Controls sit on the event and task pages (a lead-time picker, or an exact time behind a disclosure) and on Today, where the standalone form lives.
