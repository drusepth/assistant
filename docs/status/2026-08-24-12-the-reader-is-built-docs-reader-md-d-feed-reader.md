**The reader is built (docs/READER.md, D-feed-reader)**: Google Reader's shape on a box the household owns — the feeds behind your hobbies, in one chronological river. Two tables: **`feeds`** (a subscription, `Visible` + `Sectionable`, filed under the hobby lenses it belongs to) and **`feed_items`** (an entry plus the two pieces of state a reader actually needs: read, and saved). Items are deliberately **not** Sectionable — an item's tags are its feed's tags, so filing happens once, at the subscription, and the hobby filter is a join rather than a per-item write. **Paste anything**: `Feeds::Discover` rewrites the addresses whose feed is mechanical (a subreddit, a YouTube channel, a repo's releases), then tries the URL itself, then the page's `<link rel="alternate">` tags, then four conventional paths — which needed one small addition to the shared fetcher, `Web::Fetch.raw`, because finding an autodiscovery tag means reading the markup that the text extraction exists to throw away (anything that hands a page to a *model* still goes through `.text`). `Feeds::Parse` normalizes RSS 2.0, RDF, Atom and JSON Feed into one shape, forgiving by the FeedParser rule — one malformed entry is dropped, never fatal. `Feeds::Refresh` folds entries in **by guid**, so a publisher who re-lists the same item every hour can't flood the river and a re-listed item stays read; a corrected headline updates in place, but read/saved is the reader's and a fetch never touches it.

**The ranking was built and then cut, and the cut is the decision worth recording.** The first pass had a hobby-aware relevance score (the lens names, the titles of hobby-tagged items, learned token weights), up/down votes that trained it, and a decay curve balancing match against age — `relevance × 12h − hours_old`, with a whole docs section explaining why a perfect match was worth half a day of freshness. It worked, and it was wrong for what this is. **A curated list needs no sifting**: every source here was added by hand, one at a time, by the person reading it, so ranking only makes the order unpredictable — which is the property of the feeds this exists to replace. **A vote is a rating mechanic, and ratings need an audience**: one person reading their own subscriptions has nobody to signal to, and the only honest signals — did you read it, did you keep it — were already buttons. And **chronological needs no paragraph to justify it**; anything whose ordering does has already lost an argument with a river. So `Feeds::Interest` and `Feeds::Rank` are gone, `vote` and `relevance` are out of the schema (the create migration was rewritten rather than followed by a remove-column, since the table is new on this branch — anyone who already ran it wants `db:migrate:down:primary VERSION=20260824084509` before pulling), the sort chips are gone, and `Feeds::River` now pages in SQL instead of holding a ranking window in memory. What survives from that pass is the part that was never about ranking: feeds are **filed under hobby lenses**, so the river filters by hobby, exactly as every other lens in the app works. The `.btn-icon` atom added for the vote arrows went with them.

**Politeness is a design constraint**, since this is the one part of the app that polls strangers' servers on a timer: an hourly floor per feed swept every 15 minutes, exponential backoff on failure capped at a day, a longer interval for a feed nobody has read in a month, and a read-item cap per feed — saved and unread are never pruned. **Hostile-input handling** is the mail threat model applied to stranger XML: entities never expanded and no DTD fetched, summaries stripped to text at parse time so nothing downstream holds a string it could call `html_safe` on, item links and image sources refused unless `http(s)`, images lazy and referrer-free, outbound links `noopener noreferrer nofollow`, every fetch behind the existing SSRF guard.

Three surfaces: **`/reader`** (the river, newest first — unread/saved/everything, narrowed by hobby or feed, Mark-all-read scoped to whatever you're looking at, and per item save / mark read / **Make a task** prefilled and pre-tagged, which is what keeps this a hobby surface rather than a better-groomed reading list), **`/feeds`** (one paste field, unread counts, honest health — "Last check failed — the site answered HTTP 500" — and the lens picker), and a **"From your feeds"** card on the Hobbies panel showing the three newest unread. Two bugs the work found: pruning read the association's newest-first order and appended to it, so `.order` deleted exactly the wrong end (`.reorder`), and the action row broke its own labels into two-word columns on a 390pt phone. Driven end to end in a real Chromium at 1280pt and 390pt: the river in publisher order across feeds, a save re-rendering exactly one row over Turbo, and zero horizontal overflow. This also resolves the *sources* half of `docs/HOBBIES.md` §3's Q9 split honestly — the user picks the sources, so the app needs no crawler and no capability it doesn't have. Deferred and named in the doc: conditional GET, OPML import/export (subscriptions already ride the JSON export), a "what did I miss" digest as a D20 watcher, and `j`/`k` keyboard navigation, which is the one Reader affordance its actual users would notice missing.

**And then it was made dense (`.reader-line`)**: the first cut rendered each item as a card in an `.item-stack` — the app's shape for a list of six things, and the wrong one for a list of four hundred you are *scanning*. Google Reader's row is the reference and nothing since has improved on it: source column, headline, the opening words, the age, one line, at a height where twenty fit on a laptop screen (measured: every row exactly 37px, nine items in 330px where the cards took 900). Three things carry it. **Unread is the emphasised state and read the quiet one**, inverting the app's usual weighting on purpose — in a pile, what you have dealt with should recede without disappearing. **The verbs wait for the pointer**: Save / Read / Task fade in on hover on a device that can hover and on focus for a keyboard, as *opacity* rather than display, so a row can't change height under somebody scanning; a touch device, which can never hover, shows them always. **The phone drops the preview, not the headline** — below `lg` the opening words go, and the source, age, hobby and verbs share one small line, so a row is a headline plus one line rather than three; the filter rows scroll sideways instead of wrapping into four. The chrome above the list went with it: the `Ui::PageHeader` and its chunky pill row became a title line plus three lines of small text links, because on this page every pixel of furniture is a headline somebody doesn't see. `/feeds` and the Hobbies panel took the same density. One process note worth keeping: `bin/rails server` alone does **not** rebuild Tailwind (that's `bin/dev`'s watcher), so the first browser run was measuring a stale stylesheet and showed 100px rows and hover-reveal that appeared not to work — `bin/rails tailwindcss:build` after each CSS *or template* change, since the scanner reads the templates too.
