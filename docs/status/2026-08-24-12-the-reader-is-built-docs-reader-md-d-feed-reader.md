**The reader is built (docs/READER.md, D-feed-reader)**: Google Reader's shape, on a box the household owns, with the thing Reader never had — it knows what you're into. Two tables: **`feeds`** (a subscription, `Visible` + `Sectionable`, so it is filed under the hobby lenses it belongs to) and **`feed_items`** (an entry plus *this reader's* state: read, saved, vote, relevance). Items are deliberately **not** Sectionable — an item's tags are its feed's tags, so filing happens once, at the subscription, and the hobby filter is a join rather than a per-item write. **Paste anything**: `Feeds::Discover` rewrites the addresses whose feed is mechanical (a subreddit, a YouTube channel, a repo's releases), then tries the URL itself, then the page's `<link rel="alternate">` tags, then four conventional paths — which needed one small addition to the shared fetcher, `Web::Fetch.raw`, because finding an autodiscovery tag means reading the markup that the text extraction exists to throw away (anything that hands a page to a *model* still goes through `.text`). `Feeds::Parse` normalizes RSS 2.0, RDF, Atom and JSON Feed into one shape, forgiving by the FeedParser rule — one malformed entry is dropped, never fatal. `Feeds::Refresh` folds entries in **by guid**, so a publisher who re-lists the same item every hour can't flood the river and a re-listed item stays read; a corrected headline updates in place, but read/saved/voted is the reader's and is never touched by a fetch.

**The ranking is the point, and it is deliberately arithmetic**: `score = relevance × 12h − hours_old`. Everything in hours, so it is explainable in one sentence — **a perfect match is worth half a day of freshness** — a lift, never a permanent seat, which is the failure mode of ranking purely on affinity. The first shape tried was the Hacker News curve (`weight / (hours + 2)^1.35`) and it was **wrong for this**: multiplying a decay curve by affinity ties "how much does interest matter" to "how fast does news age", and at any gravity steep enough for a news river a strong hobby match was outranked by anything two hours fresher. The tests said so before the browser did. `relevance` (-1..1) is `Feeds::Interest` — the hobby lens names the user chose, the titles of what they keep under those lenses, and the votes they cast here (+0.5 up, −0.9 down: dislike learns faster, because a river's failure mode is one loud feed drowning the rest and the user has already told us), squashed through `tanh` so six weak word-hits can't outrank the hobby itself. **No model call anywhere in it** — the reader has to work on an instance with no API key at all — nothing leaves the box, every lift says why in the user's own nouns ("Because you're into Woodworking"), a downvote *sinks* an item instead of hiding it, and Newest turns the whole thing off in one click.

**Politeness is a design constraint**, since this is the one part of the app that polls strangers' servers on a timer: an hourly floor per feed swept every 15 minutes, exponential backoff on failure capped at a day, a longer interval for a feed nobody has read in a month, and a read-item cap per feed — saved and unread are never pruned. **Hostile-input handling** is the mail threat model applied to stranger XML: entities never expanded and no DTD fetched, summaries stripped to text at parse time so nothing downstream holds a string it could call `html_safe` on, item links and image sources refused unless `http(s)`, images lazy and referrer-free, outbound links `noopener noreferrer nofollow`, every fetch behind the existing SSRF guard.

Three surfaces: **`/reader`** (the river — unread/saved/everything × interesting/newest, narrowed by hobby or feed, with vote/save/read and **Make a task** prefilled and pre-tagged, which is what keeps this a hobby surface rather than a better-groomed reading list), **`/feeds`** (one paste field, unread counts, honest health — "Last check failed — the site answered HTTP 500" — and the lens picker that makes the ranking yours), and a **"From your feeds"** card on the Hobbies panel showing the three the ranking would lead with. Two bugs the work found: pruning read the association's newest-first order and appended to it, so `.order` deleted exactly the wrong end (`.reorder`), and the five-verb action row broke its own labels into two-word columns on a 390pt phone — fixed with a new `.btn-icon` atom (`.btn-touch`'s square, non-stretching sibling) for the vote arrows. Driven end to end in a real Chromium at 1280pt and 390pt: the strong match leading over a newer irrelevant one, Newest flipping it, a save re-rendering exactly one row over Turbo, and zero horizontal overflow on both the river and the Hobbies page. This also resolves the *sources* half of `docs/HOBBIES.md` §3's Q9 split honestly — the user picks the sources, so the app needs no crawler and no capability it doesn't have; what still waits on Q9 is anything the app would have to go and find on its own. Deferred and named in the doc: conditional GET, OPML import/export, and a "what did I miss" digest (a watcher over the same rows, and the natural home for the LLM garnish this deliberately does without).
