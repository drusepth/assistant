**The photo map zooms, on a coastline worth zooming into (D-travel-facet)**: at world scale London and Paris are one smudge and a trip through two cities is a single dot. Zoom is now navigation rather than a gesture — a view is a `?bbox=` URL, so it is linkable, bookmarkable, survives a reload and works with scripting off; the map page offers a zoom link per country built from that country's own pins, and a trip opens at its own box. Zooming is also what makes the clustering honest: the radius is now a distance *on screen* rather than on Earth, scaled by the window, so a pin stays the same size in the reader's eye and two cities an hour apart separate as soon as you zoom to their country. A zoomed map narrows its SQL to the box, so it reads only the photos it will draw. The 110m basemap is a blob with straight edges at that zoom, so zoomed views draw a second file: 1:50m, cut into ~10° cells that each carry a bounding box, of which only the ones in view are emitted — showing Portugal costs ~4KB of coastline instead of the ~150KB ring Natural Earth actually stores (its land is a handful of enormous polygons, and Afro-Eurasia is one). The cutting is Sutherland–Hodgman in the generator; a cell's rings share one path with `fill-rule="evenodd"` so lakes stay holes rather than becoming islands, and cells are clipped a hair oversized so neighbours overlap instead of abutting — two filled shapes meeting on an exact edge leave an anti-aliasing seam that reads as a crack across the land. Zoomed maps are lettered from the gazetteer that already names the pins: a dozen nearby settlements, biggest first, skipped where they would land on each other or on a pin, because a pin is the point and lettering is context.
