# TRAVEL.md — the Travel section

The eighth top-level section (**D-travel-facet**), added by request. It ships
with one thing in it: a world map of every geotagged photo in the owner's
library. This document is what is built, why it is built that way, and what
the hub is expected to grow.

## 1. Why a section and not a page

A trip is not a kind of item. It is a flight (Event), a passport and an
insurance policy (Documents), a budget line (Money), the people you went with
(People), a plan (Goal) and several hundred photos. The app's whole data model
exists so that this needs no new tables: sections are lenses over one shared
pool (`section_taggings`), so Travel is a seeded row and everything else it
needs already exists. Anything built as a standalone page would have had to own
copies of five primitives, and an item tagged Travel + Money would have had to
pick one.

Travel is therefore an ordinary section:

- items tag into it, and multi-tag (`Travel + Money` for the flight, `Travel +
  People` for the friend you stayed with);
- it toggles on and off in Settings like the others, and appears in the FTUE
  picker;
- it carries agent dials (`user_sections.agent_level`) whenever it gets
  watchers;
- it takes the sidebar dot and the chip colors from `SectionsHelper` (sage,
  the calm register, alternating away from Rights above it).

Two files carry the taxonomy row: `db/seeds.rb` for fresh installs, and
`20260823092731_add_travel_section.rb` for boxes already running, because
nobody re-runs seeds on an upgrade. Keep them in step.

## 2. The map

`Photos::Map` turns located photos into pins; `Ui::PhotoMapComponent` draws
them; the Travel panel and `/photos/map` are the two surfaces. Three
constraints shaped all of it.

**The basemap ships in the repo.** `app/assets/images/world_land.svg` is
generated by `script/build_world_map.rb` from Natural Earth's public-domain
1:110m land vectors, simplified (Douglas-Peucker, ~40KB) and projected to a
plate carrée 1000×500 viewBox. The projection is therefore arithmetic:

    x = (lon + 180) / 360 * 1000        y = (90 - lat) / 180 * 500

No tile server, no map SDK, no key. That is not asceticism: a tile host's
request log for a photo map *is* the owner's itinerary, which is precisely the
kind of thing this app exists not to hand to a third party — and a map that
needs the network is a map that is blank on a plane, on a locked-down box, and
the day the vendor changes its terms. The land path is inlined rather than
served as an `<img>` so it takes `currentColor` and the palette stays in the
token file (`docs/DESIGN.md`).

**Nothing is geocoded over the network, and nothing is asked of a model.**
Pin names come from `Datasets::Places` — ~7,300 public-domain settlements
shipped in `db/datasets/places.csv`, matched nearest-first in memory. A lookup
service would leak the same itinerary as a tile host; a model asked to name a
coordinate will cheerfully answer "Paris" about a photo taken in Versailles.

What a gazetteer of cities can honestly say is bounded, so the label is too:
within 20km it says "Lisbon", within 100km "Near Lisbon", and past that
nothing at all — it has never heard of the village you stayed in, and saying
so is better than guessing. Nearest wins over most famous (Kyoto stays Kyoto
next to Osaka), a missing or malformed file leaves an unnamed map rather than
an error, and every surface keeps the coordinates as its fallback. Zoomed maps
draw a few nearby settlement names from the same file as context, which is
what makes a bare coastline readable.

**The map is owner-only.** Photo coordinates are owner-only (Q14): shared
viewers get EXIF-stripped variants and the photo page hides the location. A
map of everything you have been near is exactly the surface that would hand
that back, so `Photos::Map` is constructed from `user.photos`, never
`Photo.visible_to`, and the `?near=` timeline filter narrows to your own
library too. A household member can see the photo you shared; they still
cannot see where it was taken.

### Clustering

Greedy clustering against a spatial hash, newest photo first: each photo joins
the first pin whose centre is within the clustering radius or starts one.
Cells are twice the radius, so a centroid drifts at most one cell from its
seed and scanning the 3×3 neighbourhood is exhaustive rather than merely
likely.

**The radius is a distance on screen, not on Earth.** At world zoom it is 6
map units (≈240km), chosen so London and Paris stay two pins — merging them
would be a lie about where you had been. Zoomed views scale it by the window,
which keeps a pin the same size in the reader's eye and is what finally
separates two cities an hour apart: at world zoom they share a pin, zoomed
into their country they do not.

Two pins that close still overlap at world zoom, and that is accepted rather
than fixed: the count, the dates and the link ride each pin (and the Places
list under the map), a solid ring keeps overlapping pins reading as two
objects, smaller pins draw last so a single photo never hides behind a city,
and zoom is the way out.

### The window, and zoom

The world view crops the empty polar bands by default (82°N to 58°S) — but
only as a default. A pin outside that band pushes the window back out rather
than falling off the edge of its own map, which is the difference between a
crop and a lie. The container's `aspect-ratio` follows the computed window, so
the map is never letterboxed or squashed.

**Zoom is navigation, not a gesture.** A view is a `?bbox=south,west,north,east`
URL: the map page offers one zoom link per country (built from that country's
own pins), a trip opens at its own box, and the result is a page like any
other — linkable, bookmarkable, and working with scripting off. A requested
box is padded so pins never sit on the edge, never narrower than `MIN_SPAN`
(a day in one city is a box a few hundred metres across, and blown up to fill
a card that is a meaningless smear of coastline), and shaped by *growing* the
short side — cropping would drop a photo out of a view that claims to hold it.
A zoomed map also narrows its SQL to the box, so it reads only the photos it
is going to draw.

### Two basemaps

At country zoom the 110m outline is a blob with straight edges, so zoomed
views draw a second file: `world_land_detail.svg`, 1:50m, cut into ~10° grid
cells that each carry their own bounding box. The component emits only the
cells the window touches — showing Portugal costs ~4KB of coastline instead
of the ~150KB ring that Natural Earth actually stores (its land is a handful
of enormous polygons; Afro-Eurasia is one). Cutting happens in the generator,
where each ring is clipped (Sutherland–Hodgman) to every cell it touches; a
cell's rings share one path with `fill-rule="evenodd"` so lakes stay holes
instead of becoming islands, and cells are clipped a hair oversized so
neighbours overlap rather than abut — two filled shapes meeting on an exact
edge leave a one-pixel anti-aliasing seam that reads as a crack across the
land.

Zoomed maps are also **lettered** from the gazetteer: up to a dozen nearby
settlement names, biggest first, skipped where they would land on each other
or on a pin. A coastline with nothing written on it is a shape; the same
coastline with "Porto" and "Coimbra" on it is a map. The world view gets none
— at that scale a dozen city names is noise on top of the pins that matter.

### No JavaScript

A pin is an `<a>` to `/photos?near=lat,lon`, which is the ordinary timeline
filtered to that place — the grid, the lightbox, month grouping and pagination
already exist there, so the map navigates into the app instead of growing a
second photo grid. Nothing on the map needs scripting, and the Places list
under it is the same set of destinations for anyone navigating by keyboard or
screen reader.

## 3. Trips

`Travel::Trips` finds the trips already sitting in the library, and the hub
offers them. A trip is one of the few things a life assistant can work out on
its own without guessing: photos carry where and when, so "away from home for
more than a day" is a fact rather than an inference. Nothing is asked of the
user and no model is called — the app proposes, the user keeps the ones that
were really trips.

**Home** is the pin with the most distinct *days*, not the most photos: one
wedding can out-shoot a year of ordinary evenings, and the place you keep
coming back to is the place you live. Photos more than 100km from home are
"away"; away days no more than three blank days apart are one run; a run of at
least 3 photos over at least 2 days is a trip. The thresholds are deliberately
generous — a day out two towns over is not a trip, and calling it one would
bury the real ones.

A candidate's **name** comes from the gazetteer: one place is that place, a
handful in one country is the country ("Portugal" beats "Lisbon and Porto and
Faro"), anything else is the two biggest joined. Somewhere the gazetteer has
never heard of gets its month, which is at least true. It is a suggestion —
the trip page renames.

**A saved `Trip` is a name, a date range and a box on the map, and nothing
else.** Membership is derived from those three: its photos are the ones taken
inside the box while it ran, so photos imported later join the trip on their
own, and its events are simply what was on the calendar those days. That is
what keeps Q17 (person↔item links, and by extension item↔item links) deferred
— a trip needs no join table, because dates and a box already say what belongs
to it. Events read through `visible_to`, so a flight your spouse shared with
you appears on your trip; photos never do (see the owner-only rule in §2).

Saving re-derives everything server-side from the user's own photos: the form
sends only the start date it is accepting, so a hand-edited request can move a
trip's dates but can never invent a place someone never went. Dates stay
editable afterwards, because the detector's guess at when a trip started is
only as good as when the first photo was taken, and the flight out often has
none.

Trips are **owner-only**, like the map and for the same reason: a trip is a
lens on photo coordinates, and sharing one would hand a household member the
locations the photo pages withhold. The per-album "share locations" toggle
(Q14's open half) is the decision that would change this.

## 4. What the hub does not have yet

Deliberately unbuilt, in rough order of expected demand:

1. **Travel documents.** Passport and visa expiry are exactly renewal-radar's
   beat (`Watcher::RenewalRadar` already keys off "renew"/"expir"), so this may
   be a keyword and a panel rather than new machinery.
2. **Tools.** Nothing Tier-A yet without live data (Q9): a packing list from a
   trip's dates and destination is honest; "find flights" is not.
3. **Trips that know more than their dates.** A trip could carry the documents
   and notes filed during it, but that is the Q17 link table — wait for a
   second consumer to want it, per `docs/PEOPLE.md` §9.8.
4. **A per-album "share locations" toggle** — Q14's open half, and the thing
   that would let a trip be shared. Worth asking only once someone actually
   wants a household trip to carry its map.
