**D-career-signal. Career signal climbs a ladder: granted data first, manual logging always, inference never.**
The section's substrate is a **comp timeline** plus a **win log**, fed from three rungs. Rung 1, granted data: pay stubs are ordinary vault Documents and the analysis pipeline grows career-aware extraction (employer, rate, gross/net, hours, overtime, deductions) — a rate change against the previous stub lands as a comp-change Event automatically with provenance named (D16: the assistant does the filing; ambiguous reads become suggestions per D20); W-2s give annual ground truth; the LinkedIn official data export bootstraps the work-history profile and People; payroll/application email joins when the D26 IMAP consumer lands; bank-deposit signal joins when Money grows a transactions layer. Rung 2, the always-available floor: one-tap **comp-change Events** ("Raise: $19 → $21/hr") and one-line **win-log Notes** (quick-captured per D16) — manual and extracted entries form one timeline, manual winning conflicts; the win log stays manual-first because accomplishments live in the user's head before any system. Rung 3 is where Q8's actual lesson lives: the app never *guesses* — every nudge cites its source, and no granted or logged signal means no nudge, only a one-time invitation to start. Pay data is maximally sensitive, so D9 does the work: private by default, unshared comp never read cross-user, exportable and deletable like everything owned. No schema beyond extraction storage; tools read it all through `section_context`. Full design in `docs/CAREER.md`. — *Provisional until stub extraction accuracy and the capture habit prove out.*
