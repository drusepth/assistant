**D-standing-reminders. A reminder can repeat: the recurrence lives on the Reminder, never on Event or Task, and `deliver_at` stays absolute as the *next* occurrence.**
D-reminders built one-shot delivery and the whole app had no way to say *every* — not on reminders, not on events (the ICS importer flattens recurring events on purpose), not on tasks. "Take the trash out every Thursday night" is the most ordinary reminder a household has and the one shape the feature couldn't hold. Three homes were possible. **The Reminder** wins because it is already the object whose job is "tell me at a time" and the dispatcher already ticks: a *standing* reminder is a reminder with a `rule`, `deliver_at` keeps meaning one absolute indexed moment — now the next one — and delivering advances it instead of tombstoning, in the **same conditional `update_all`** that claims the row, so there is no window between "sent" and "re-armed" and a concurrent job or an intervening edit finds nothing to claim. **Not Event**: calendar recurrence (moved instances, exceptions, "this and following") is a tarpit the importer already declined, and a trash reminder isn't a calendar event. **Not Task**, though it was the tempting one: a task's invariant is a clear done/not-done state, and a task that respawns either piles up a row per week or trips the staleness markers the Tasks page built to defuse the graveyard; recurring *tasks* are a separate question (Q-routines) that a standing reminder on an ordinary Task doesn't close off — "reminders on tasks with a cadence" already falls out, with no respawn engine. The rule is **RFC 5545 `RRULE` text**, because the importer already parses and expands that subset with DST-safe arithmetic: it moves into `Recurrence::Rule`, one grammar tested once, and the picker composes rules explicitly (`BYDAY`, `BYMONTHDAY`) so the next occurrence is a pure function of rule, current occurrence, and household zone — no anchor column. A standing reminder is absolute-only (a lead time trails one item's one date; nothing to repeat over), rolls forward on create rather than refusing a first occurrence that has passed, and **skips missed occurrences without pinging**: `next_after(Time.current)`, not `next_after(deliver_at)`, so a box that was off for three Thursdays sends zero late reminders and the series survives — the same judgment D-reminders applies to a stale one-shot, per occurrence, keeping the row. `delivered_at` remains the one-shot tombstone a standing reminder never sets, so `pending`/`due` and the index are untouched. The picker is a **Repeat** select beside the date and time, with a row of weekday chips for the weekly repeats — the date is where the series starts, not a day you have to hunt for — and every row opens in place to be changed. Plan: `docs/REMINDERS_PLAN.md`. Three corollaries landed with it: an **Until** day rides the rule and a spent series is refused; finishing a Task retires its one-shot reminders and keeps its standing ones (done once is not the chore over); and **plain words** fill the form through the household's model — `Reminders::Parse`, one call validated into the closed sets, returning the prefilled form and saving nothing, gone without a model. — *Built: `Recurrence::Rule` (the importer delegates), `rule`/`last_delivered_at` on reminders, the advancing dispatcher, the one form on Today and the item pages with chips and Until, `update`, `reminders#parse`, the `.chip-toggle` atom.*
