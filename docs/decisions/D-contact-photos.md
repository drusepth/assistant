**D-contact-photos. Everyone in the address book gets a face, including the imported ones.**
D-user-avatars deferred exactly this — "contact photos are a Person feature and would need their own field" — and drew the line at inference: a contact linked to a household account wears that member's photo, a plain contact keeps initials, and nothing is ever guessed from a name. That line holds; what changes is that a contact can now *be given* a photo. `Avatarable` is the D-user-avatars attachment lifted out of `User` whole — one `has_one_attached :avatar`, one 200×200 WEBP `:thumb` variant, the same content-type and size guardrails, the same initials fallback — and `Person` includes it, so a contact and a member are the same kind of thing to every surface that draws a face. `Ui::AvatarComponent` gains a `person:` argument and one precedence rule: **the photo you gave them beats the one they'd inherit**, initials beat a borrowed face, so a contact linked to Bob's account shows Bob until you upload something for the *contact*. The upload sits at the top of the person form (removal is `DELETE /people/:id/avatar`, mirroring the member's own-photo-only route — always one of your own contacts, never a shared one). The half that matters at scale is **import**: address-book exports already carry the photo, so the vCard parser reads inline base64 (2.1/3.0) and 4.0 `data:` URIs, the phone's Contact Picker path serializes its icon Blob the same way, and a photo given only as an `https` URI is skipped on purpose — fetching it would be a third-party call on every import, which is the mistake gravatar-style lookup was refused for. A malformed or oversized photo costs its contact a face, never the import. Photos leave with the user in the D14 export (`contact_photos/`, originals). Not built: cropping (upload a square, or accept the centre crop), face detection from the photo library, and any push of a photo back to a CardDAV server — that link stays read-only (D26). — *Built.*
