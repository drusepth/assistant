**D-reminders-on-tasks. A reminder is always set on a task or an event, and a task's reminder and repeat are fields on the task, not a second list.** Amends **D-reminders**, which made "remind me to call the pharmacy at 4pm", hung on nothing, the case the feature existed for. In use that produced two overlapping lists. Today showed a Reminders card beside the tasks, and routines (D-task-routines) appeared in it as reminders "on this task", with a Remove button that quietly ended the repeat. A household member asked the question that settles it: *what would I set a reminder for that I wouldn't set a task for?* Nothing. The pharmacy call is a task you want a nudge about, and after the nudge it belongs on the list until it's done. So:

- **A task has three schedule fields**: *Due*, *Repeat*, and *Remind me at*. They sit together on every task form, including the quick-add, where they are folded under "Due date, repeat, reminder". A time with no repeat is one reminder on the due day, and a time with no due date means today. A repeat is the routine, with its ping at that time (9am if blank). The rows read the fields back as chips (↻ "Mon, Wed, Fri · 9 PM", 🔔 "4 PM"), and the task page shows them in its Details. Nobody manages the reminder row as a thing of its own.
- **The same-day reminder is stored as a lead time counted from the start of the due day.** For times after `Remindable::DAY_START_HOUR` that lead is negative (`Reminder.lead_for_time_of_day`). Stored that way it follows the task when it is rescheduled, and retires when the date is cleared, through the `resync!` path "an hour before" already uses, with no second sync to keep. Leads that land on an earlier day ("the day before") are not the form's; they stay listed on the task page as "More reminders".
- **A repeating task is due on its occurrence.** Setting or changing a repeat moves the due date to the first occurrence. When the routine fires, `come_round!(on:)` sets the due date to the day it came round for. The chore therefore appears under *Due today* on its day, and a missed one shows as overdue rather than disappearing. Re-saving the form leaves an owed, overdue chore where it is. Only a new rule or a moved due date restarts the series.
- **`Reminder` validates the presence of its subject.** The Today page loses its "+ Reminder" button and its Reminders card. Events keep their Reminders card ("an hour before", or an exact time), without a Repeat picker. A repeating ping about a one-off event was never a shape anyone needed, and an existing one still opens on its repeat so it can be changed or ended.
- **Plain words move to the new-task page.** `Reminders::Parse` fills the task form ("take the trash out every Thursday night" gives a title, a first day, a weekly repeat and 20:00). As before, nothing is saved until the click (D20), and the box is off, saying so, without a model.
- **Migration.** Every pending standalone reminder became a task carrying it. A repeating one became that task's routine, due on its next occurrence. A one-shot became "remind me at <time>" on a task due that day. Routines already on undated tasks got their occurrence as the due date. Delivered reminders are history and were left alone.

*Why not keep standalone reminders for the "just ping me" case.* Two ways to write down the same intention means choosing between them every time, and then finding the thing in whichever list you chose. A ping with no task behind it also can't tell you whether you did the thing. The cost is one extra field on the task form, and it is folded away until wanted.
