**D-reminders-on-tasks. A reminder is always set on a task or an event, and a task's reminder and repeat are fields on the task, not a second list.** Amends **D-reminders**, which made "remind me to call the pharmacy at 4pm", hung on nothing, the case the feature existed for. In use that produced two overlapping lists. Today showed a Reminders card beside the tasks, and routines (D-task-routines) appeared in it as reminders "on this task", with a Remove button that quietly ended the repeat. A household member asked the question that settles it: *what would I set a reminder for that I wouldn't set a task for?* Nothing. The pharmacy call is a task you want a nudge about, and after the nudge it belongs on the list until it's done. So:

- **A task has three schedule fields**: *Due*, *Repeat*, and *Remind me at*. They sit together on every task form, including the quick-add, where they are folded under "Due date, repeat, reminder". A time with no due date means today. The rows read the fields back as chips (↻ "Mon, Wed, Fri · 🔔 9 PM", 🔔 "4 PM"), and the task page shows them in its Details. Nobody manages the reminder row as a thing of its own.
- **The schedule is the task's own.** `tasks.repeat_rule` (RRULE text, composed from the picker by the same `Reminder.compose_rule` a reminder uses) and `tasks.remind_time` ("16:00") are columns on the task. Underneath is exactly one reminder row marked `schedule: true` (`Task#schedule_reminder`), which `Task#sync_schedule_reminder` keeps in step with the task's fields on every save. No code has to guess which reminder the form owns, and nothing is stored in a field meant for something else. Reminders set by hand from the task page are `schedule: false` (`Reminder.by_hand`) and are left alone.
- **Repeat and reminder are independent.** A repeat with no time is a *silent* routine: its schedule reminder (`reminders.silent`) fires at the start of the day, brings the chore back onto the board, and sends nothing. A time with no repeat is one reminder at that time on the due day, re-armed when the task is rescheduled. A time already past, typed into the form for today, is a field error rather than a reminder dropped without a word. The Today/Tomorrow buttons moving a task whose ping already went are never an error.
- **A repeating task is due on its occurrence.** A new or changed rule starts on the first day, on or after the due day, that fits the rule. Today's chore is therefore on today's list even when today's ping hour has passed; the ping waits for the next occurrence. When the routine fires, `come_round!(on:)` sets the due date to the day it came round for, so a missed one reads as overdue. Moving a repeating task's due day ("do it tomorrow") moves this time round and leaves the series alone. Changing the time keeps the series on the occurrence it is waiting for. Completing a repeating task leaves it done until its next day comes round (D-task-routines' fixed schedule), rather than jumping it forward the way Todoist does, because what you did today should stay visible as done today.
- **`Reminder` validates the presence of its subject.** The Today page loses its "+ Reminder" button and its Reminders card. Events keep their Reminders card ("an hour before", or an exact time), without a Repeat picker. A repeating ping about a one-off event was never a shape anyone needed, and an existing one still opens on its repeat so it can be changed or ended.
- **The Calendar draws tasks, not reminders.** Its layers are Events and Tasks; the Reminders layer and the day view's "Set a reminder" form (both added the same day, before this) are gone. A task with a reminder time sits at that time on its day, led by a bell, and only for its owner. A repeating task is drawn once as its row, on its due day, and again on each day ahead that its rule will bring it round, as a quieter dashed chip led by the repeat arrows. That chip links to the task and can't be dragged, since there is nothing to move until the day comes. An event's own reminders stay on the event page. Drawing them on the grid beside the event put the same appointment on the calendar twice.
- **Plain words move to the new-task page.** `Reminders::Parse` fills the task form ("take the trash out every Thursday night" gives a title, a first day, a weekly repeat and 20:00). As before, nothing is saved until the click (D20), and the box is off, saying so, without a model.
- **Migration** (`MoveTaskScheduleOntoTasks`). Every pending standalone reminder became a task carrying it as its schedule reminder: a repeating one with its rule and time on the task, a one-shot as a reminder time on a task due that day. Every routine already on a task moved its rule and time onto the task, and an undated one got its next occurrence as the due date. Delivered reminders are history and were left alone.

*Why not keep standalone reminders for the "just ping me" case.* Two ways to write down the same intention means choosing between them every time, and then finding the thing in whichever list you chose. A ping with no task behind it also can't tell you whether you did the thing. The cost is one extra field on the task form, and it is folded away until wanted.
