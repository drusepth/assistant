**D-food-catalog. A kitchen's foods are rows; the model files each new ingredient onto one once, and every match after that is a join.**
Amends D-pantry's matching over `item_key` and its "small static table" of aisles, both of which are D-no-keyword-logic's word list by another name: "AP flour", "plain flour" and "all-purpose flour" are three keys and one food, "chicken thighs" satisfies a recipe's "chicken" and not the reverse, and the failure is the silent kind — "what can I make" quietly misses the recipe because the fridge said scallions and the recipe said spring onions. So recipe ingredients, pantry items and list items each gain a `food_id` into a per-kitchen `foods` table (name, aisle, default location, one-level `kind_of`, allergens from the nine-major closed set, `merged_into`), with `food_aliases` mapping each normalised key to its food. `ResolveFoodsJob` asks the model once, batched, over the kitchen's existing foods — which is this line, or is it new, and if new its aisle, location, kind and allergens — and validates every answer into `Food::AISLES`, `Food::ALLERGENS` and rows that exist. **Ask about less**: a key resolves once per kitchen, ever; after the first month nearly every line is an alias hit. The user's merges and edits are their own explicit rules, outside D-no-keyword-logic's reach. **With no model**, each new key becomes its own food, so matching is exact-typed-text only — it misses, never mis-matches — the fridge page says so in one line, and aisle grouping is off rather than guessed. Allergens only ever *exclude*, are shown with "check the packet", and never make a dish "safe". The catalog is kitchen vocabulary, but its page lists only foods reached through a row the viewer can see, and recipes (user-owned) resolve against the *viewer's* kitchen through aliases at match time. — *Built 2026-09-23; F3 of `docs/FOOD_PLAN.md`.*

*As built (2026-09-23): no `recipe_ingredients.food_id` and no `merged_into` — a recipe line resolves through the viewer's kitchen's aliases at match time, and a merge moves aliases and rows then deletes the merged food. Added `foods.staple` ("we always have it": water, salt, oil), proposed by the model and toggled by the household. See `docs/FOOD_PLAN.md` §9.*
