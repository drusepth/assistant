**D-pronouns. One closed set of pronouns — she, he, they — that everything the app writes a sentence about carries, and that every model surface is told.**
The app writes sentences *about* people and animals all day: a flash, a card subtitle, a nudge, a reorder alert, and — the part that matters most — the drafts a model writes and the user then sends to that very person. Every one of those needs a pronoun, and every one of them had one hardcoded. The pets surface said *her* about every animal. The people surface was luckier and mostly stayed neutral, but its **model** surfaces were not: `person_context` sent a name, a relationship and the user's notes and no pronoun at all, so a model wrote a reconnect message to "Rosa" and picked a pronoun off the name — a guess dressed as knowledge, landing in a message the user is about to send. `gift_ideas` went further and *taught* the guess, its worked example reading "[ask her sister] which authors she's into lately", which would have out-argued any pronoun the record later supplied. **A pronoun that doesn't reach the model doesn't reach the user.** So: one top-level `Pronouns` value object (subject, object, possessive, reflexive, plus `verb("eats", "eat")` and `is` for the agreement "they" forces, and `slashed` for the one line every context builder now sends), and a `Pronounced` concern over a `pronoun` column. Three keys, and **blank is a real answer that reads as "they"** — not an unfinished form, and what someone who'd rather not say gets by leaving the field alone. Where the pronoun comes from differs by record and that is the whole design: a **`Person` is asked outright**, because a contact record has no business asking anyone's sex and pronouns are the thing the copy actually needs; a **`Pet` derives it from the `sex` already on file**, because a form that asks a household for a cat's sex and then for the cat's pronouns is written for a power user and the target user is not one — with a normally-blank `pronoun` override beside it, so the household that calls their male cat "they" is not argued with. Free text could never have done either job: reading prose for a pronoun is keyword-matching, which D-no-keyword-logic rules out, so `pets.sex` became a closed set in the same change and spayed/neutered moved to its own flag, being a status rather than a sex. `User` deliberately gets nothing: no copy in the app writes a pronoun about a household member (it addresses them as "you" and otherwise uses their name), there is no profile view to set one in, and a column no sentence reads is a field that goes stale unnoticed — the trigger to revisit is the first piece of copy or the first prompt that writes about a member in the third person. — *Built 2026-09, pets first and then generalized. What the generalization found: the pets pass had put the pronoun into `pet_context` from the start, so the people gap was invisible until the same question was asked of `person_context`, which is the argument for doing this per-mechanism rather than per-page.*
