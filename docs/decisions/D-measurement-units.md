**D-measurement-units. Pounds and miles are the norm — stored, shown and typed; kilograms and kilometres are a per-user exception**
The people who run this app are American, so imperial is not a display preference layered over metric storage — it is what the columns hold: `pets.weight_lb`, `places.radius_mi` (whole miles, default 25), `happenings.distance_mi`. The vet-sheet landing asks the model for pounds, `Place#distance_to` answers in miles, and the Ticketmaster search is sent in miles. A user who wants kilograms and kilometres picks them in Settings (`users.measurement_system`, default `imperial`), and `Units` converts for that user alone, both ways — the figure a page prints and the figure a form posts — so no view carries a conversion factor. Forms post `weight` / `radius` in the reader's unit; `weight_lb` / `radius_mi` are still accepted directly. A metric weight round-trips exactly (the column's 0.01 lb is finer than the 0.01 kg shown); a metric radius snaps to the nearest whole mile, the column's grain, which is fine for "counts as here". Internal geometry that no one reads — the gazetteer's snap thresholds, the trip "away from home" test — stays in whatever unit its haversine uses. Pet-food amounts are untouched: each row already carries its own unit (`PetFood::UNITS`).
