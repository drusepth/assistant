**D-pet-food-shelf. Pet food is structured, because unlike medications it computes.**
D36 made a medication a `Note` with a kind, on the grounds that a med list is prose nobody does arithmetic on. Food is the opposite case and earns real columns: `pet_foods` rows (pet, name, brand, form, amount per day, package size, package cost, `started_on`, `ended_on`) exist so that two numbers can be *derived* rather than guessed — package ÷ daily amount says when the bag runs out, so "reorder around 14 March" is subtraction, and accepting it writes an all-day Event, which means the renewal radar gives it lead time and the calendar shows it with no new reminder machinery; and the summed monthly cost of active foods is a real target for the Pets envelope, offered to the budget and never written over a target set by hand (D16). Ending a food rather than editing it keeps a **diet history**, which is the actual deliverable: "what is she eating, and since when?" is the first question at every vet visit and the whole content of an elimination trial. What it refuses to do is compute portions from breed and weight, count calories, or rate a food — those are a vet's numbers and a twenty-year internet argument; the bag states a feeding amount and the app can hold *the bag's* number. The app tracks what the owner decided and what it costs. — *Built 2026-08 (W3), with the reorder watcher in W5. One thing the build changed: the plan counted a bag from `started_on`, which drifts the reorder date a bag further out every month — `opened_on` is its own column, blank is allowed, and blank means the app makes no reorder claim at all rather than a wrong one. The form prefills today where the value is on screen and can be cleared: a default the user can see beats a default behind their back.*
