**D-undo-flash. A move that takes something off the list says where it went and carries its own undo**
D-row-send-off made Today's reschedule buttons *feel* like something happened, but the row still left without saying where it went, and a mis-tap — the easiest mistake on this page, two buttons under one thumb — cost a hunt through Tasks to put right. Rescheduling now sets a notice naming the task and the day it landed on, and a second flash key carries the way back: `flash[:undo] = {"label" =>, "url" =>}`, rendered by `Ui::FlashComponent` as a button inside the notice rather than as a banner of its own (it joins `celebrate` in `SILENT`, the keys that ride the flash as data rather than as a sentence to read). **The undo needs no new action**: it is a reschedule to where the task was, so `to` grew from the two words the buttons send to also take an ISO date and `"none"` — the last of which is what makes an undated task undoable at all, since "today"/"tomorrow" cannot express "no date". Anything unparseable still means today, which is what this action did for every unrecognised `to` before. The banner is a `role="status"` live region, and that is the part that matters beyond convenience: on a page that morphs on redirect-to-self the sentence is *inserted* into a loaded page, so a screen reader announces it — making this the only account of what happened available to anyone who can't watch the row animate away. (A live region present at first page load is not announced; that is a browser rule, not something worked around here.) — *Built: `TasksController#reschedule` with `reschedule_target` and `due_phrase`, the `undo` key in `Ui::FlashComponent`, and the flash banner's flex layout. The undo is generic — any action that can name a URL back to its previous state can set the key.*
