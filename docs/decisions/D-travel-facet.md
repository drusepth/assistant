**D-travel-facet. Travel is the eighth section, and it opens on a map the app draws itself.**
The product's seven sections were finalised in CLAUDE.md; Travel is the first addition to them, added by request, and it is a section rather than a page because the alternative fails the app's own model — a trip is a flight (Event), a passport (Document), a budget (Money), the people you went with, and a pile of photos, so anything that isn't a lens over the shared pool would have to own copies of five primitives. As a section it costs one seeded row and inherits tagging, sharing, agent dials, Settings toggles and the browse zone for free.

Its lead panel (D37) is the photo map, and three choices inside it are the ones worth keeping. **The basemap ships in the repo**: `app/assets/images/world_land.svg` is generated by `script/build_world_map.rb` from public-domain Natural Earth vectors, and pins are positioned by arithmetic (plate carrée, matching the SVG's viewBox), so drawing a map of your own life costs zero requests to anybody. A tile host is the wrong dependency twice over — it breaks offline and on a locked-down box, and the request log of a photo map *is* the itinerary. **Nothing is geocoded**: a pin says how many photos and which months, never a place name, because naming a coordinate needs either a lookup service (see above) or a model guessing, and a confidently wrong "Paris" is worse than an honest dot. **The map is owner-only**, which is Q14 held rather than quietly widened: a shared photo already arrives with its EXIF stripped, and a map is precisely the surface that would hand its location back — so the household member who can see the photo still cannot see where it was taken, and following a pin filters your own library only.

The map is also deliberately dumb about *zoom*: there is none. Clustering merges photos within ~240km, which keeps London and Paris as two pins, and two nearby pins are simply allowed to overlap the way they would on any world map — the count, the dates and the link live on each pin and in the Places list beneath it. A pin is an ordinary link to the timeline filtered to that place, so the whole surface works with JavaScript off.

The hub then took four upgrades in one pass, and the shape of all four is the same argument as the basemap: **when a map wants data, ship the data.** *Names* come from a 7,300-settlement public-domain gazetteer on the D-public-datasets layer, matched in memory, hedged to what a gazetteer of cities can honestly say — "Lisbon" inside 20km, "Near Lisbon" inside 100km, nothing beyond. *Trips* are read out of the photos rather than asked for: home is the cluster with the most distinct days (one wedding out-shoots a year of evenings), away is 100km, and a saved trip is a name, a date range and a box — its photos and events derive from those, which is what keeps Q17 deferred. *Zoom* is a `?bbox=` URL rather than a gesture, with the clustering radius measured in rendered pixels so London and Paris separate exactly when you zoom to their country, over a second 1:50m basemap cut into cells at generation time so showing Portugal costs 4KB instead of the enormous ring Natural Earth actually stores. And the whole thing was *measured* rather than assumed (`script/benchmark_photo_map.rb`): the Travel page was 2,550ms on a 60,000-photo library, mostly because a plain `pluck` was building a BigDecimal per coordinate and a TimeWithZone per photo, and because the clustering grid's hash default block was storing an array for every cell it merely probed. 706ms now, still with no cache — and one plausible-sounding optimisation reverted for measuring slower, which is written down so nobody re-derives it. — *Built: `Photos::Map`, `Ui::PhotoMapComponent`, `Datasets::Places`, `Travel::Trips`, `Trip`, `/photos/map`, the `?near=` and `?bbox=` views, and `Sections::Panels::Travel`. Travel documents, tools and a shareable trip (Q14's open half) are deliberately still unbuilt; they land as more panels on this page. See `docs/TRAVEL.md`.*
