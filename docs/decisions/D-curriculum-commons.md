**D-curriculum-commons. Core subjects ship as an open curriculum library in the repo; enrolling instantiates a personal tree; AI-generated subjects are the same shape through a second door.**
"Replace the public school system" can't mean the model improvises K-12 per household (coverage unauditable, errors invisible) and can't mean bundling someone else's courseware (not licensable, not editable, not ours). The skeleton is plain YAML in the repo — `db/curriculum/*.yml`, the **curriculum commons**: core subjects (numeracy through calculus, reading & writing, the sciences, civics, financial literacy, digital literacy…) as strand → topic outlines with per-topic summaries stating what mastering it means, informed by public standards (Common Core / NGSS and international cousins) but jurisdiction-neutral, and terse enough that a parent reads a course in five minutes, diffs it on GitHub, and edits it in a text editor. Community packs eventually, first-party only for now (D11). Enrolling *copies* the outline into the learner's own `Subject`/`Topic` tree; a personalization pass (model, when configured) reframes summaries through the learner's interest frame and marks what placement says is already known — with no model configured the commons outline *is* the subject, and enrollment, browsing, and manual mastery marking all work dry (the D20 discipline from day one). "Learn anything" is the same shape through a second door: a free-typed topic generates a tree instead of copying one, the learner owns and edits it from there — one schema, two authors, and every downstream feature (mastery, reviews, tutor context, records) indifferent to which door a subject came through. Full design in `EDUCATION_PLAN.md`. — *Designed; W1 builds it.*
