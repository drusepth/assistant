**D-decision-links. Notifications carry a signed, expiring decision link; the link identifies, the session authorizes.**
The Q-sms-replies question, resolved toward its signed-link design with one honest simplification: once the decision is authenticated by a real session (which that entry already concluded it must be), the link needs to *identify*, not *authorize* — and accept/dismiss are idempotent, so single-use ceremony buys nothing and a replayed link is harmless. Every notification batch appends one URL, `/assistant/decisions/:token`: a purpose-scoped signed token over the batch's suggestion ids, expiring in 7 days, no new table. The page requires sign-in — a leaked or carrier-logged URL renders nothing but the sign-in redirect (the D22 lock-screen rule, extended to the link the lock screen carries) — then shows the session's user their still-actionable suggestions with full bodies and one-tap accept/snooze/dismiss. Works identically on all five channel kinds because it is just a URL in the payload (counting toward `TEXT_LIMIT` on the phone kinds); works behind NAT on every provider including the gammu modem, which is why reply-to-answer inbound parsing stays rejected (publicly reachable endpoint, spoofable sender ID). Design in `docs/AGENT_PLAN.md` §5. Resolves Q-sms-replies. — *Built (A3), with one change forced by measurement: Rails' `expires_in:`/`purpose:` metadata wrapper more than doubled the token (a 212-character URL against a 300-character SMS bound), so the payload is packed by hand and the key stays purpose-derived. At 124 characters the URL spends from the D-sms-channels budget rather than extending it — the summary yields, the link goes in whole. Self-hosted instances answer with `APP_HOST`; without one there is no link rather than a link to a domain we don't own.*
