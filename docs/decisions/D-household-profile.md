**D-household-profile. The household is a first-class object with its own page and its own dashboard hero.**
Two halves of one idea. (1) **A household profile page** (`/household`, admin-edited under the D15 split, readable by everyone) holds the facts that belong to the house rather than to a person: name, tagline, banner, mailing address, `region` (D34, unchanged and still the only jurisdiction signal), phone, own-vs-rent, move-in date, and time zone — plus the member roster and invite links, which moved off personal Settings, where they never belonged. The bar for a field is a **named consumer**, not completeness: address + phone feed `Tool::Base#sender_address_block` (opt-in per tool — a street address is the most identifying thing here, so it reaches the model only where a letterhead needs it; deposit-demand is the first), `time_zone` sets `Time.zone` for every request and job (before this, "due today" flipped over mid-evening for most of the planet), own-vs-rent decides which housing help is relevant, and move-in date drives the anniversary and lease-clock math. Address stays **free text on the D34 argument** — the consumer is a prompt and a letterhead, not a join, and the audience is global self-hosters whose address shapes don't agree. (2) **The dashboard leads with a household hero** that absorbs the old page header: greeting, household name, tagline, member avatars, and up to three *highlights* — a circle birthday (D-people-dashboard scoping: an imported address book must not become a birthday firehose), the home anniversary, an on-this-day photo from the library. `Households::Hero` computes all of it from owned data — no LLM call, no network, nothing to configure before it says something warm — and the rule for a highlight is that it must be **true today and about this household**: no streaks, no scores, no manufactured milestones, and a quiet day simply greets you rather than filling the space. Deliberately not stored: household income and size (the benefits screener asks, and both are sensitive), landlord and utility contacts (those are People and Documents), and a home-inventory blob (that's Notes) — each would have been a field with no consumer. — *Built.*
