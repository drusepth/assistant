**D-reminders. A reminder is user-armed scheduled delivery over the items that already exist — absolute or trailing the thing it hangs on, and never subject to the agent dials.**
The phone channels (D-sms-channels) could alert but not be *asked* to. Reminders close that half, and the shape falls out of two observations. First, a reminder owns no content, so it is **not a primitive**: `reminders` is a polymorphic row over an Event or a Task — or over **nothing at all**, which is the "remind me to call the pharmacy at 4pm" case the feature exists for and the one a `reminder_at` column on Event could never have served. Second, `deliver_at` is **always absolute**, even for one set as "an hour before", so the dispatcher stays a single indexed query; the lead time is re-applied by a `Remindable` hook whenever the item moves, because "an hour before" becomes a lie the moment an appointment is rescheduled, and one reminder firing at the old time is enough to stop someone trusting the feature at all. An item whose date is cleared retires its relative reminders rather than firing at a moment that no longer exists. Delivery **polls every five minutes** (`Reminders::DispatchJob.dispatch_due`, the `Email::DigestJob.dispatch_due` shape) rather than enqueueing a job at `deliver_at`: a queued job can't cleanly be un-queued when the user edits or deletes the reminder, whereas a poll reads the current truth each tick. It claims each row with an atomic conditional update before sending, since the timer can otherwise enqueue the same reminder twice and on the SMS channels a double send costs real money; and it **retires anything more than a day late** instead of delivering it, because a ping about a meeting that already happened reads as the app being broken. Times are composed and rendered in the household's zone explicitly, not in `Time.zone` — that is the household zone only inside a request, and the dispatcher is a job. The rule that matters most: a reminder **ignores the D22 section dials entirely**. Those dials say how chatty the *agent* may be; a person who set an alarm for 4pm has already answered that question. Same stance as the email plan's user-armed boomerang — no model in the path, no dial in the way, and the message code-built from the user's own words plus a clock reading. The per-channel daily cap still applies, because that is the user's own stated budget. — *Built: the `reminders` table, `Remindable` on Event and Task, the five-minute dispatcher, and the controls on the item pages and Today. Resolves Q-scheduled-reminders.*
