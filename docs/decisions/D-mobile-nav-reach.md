**D-mobile-nav-reach. On a phone the drawer gets a thumb-reachable button; the edge swipe is a shortcut layered on top, never the way in.**
The nav drawer's only opener was the hamburger in the sticky header — correct as the place an eye looks for a menu, and the worst place on a 900-point screen for the hand holding it, which is the whole of the complaint. The obvious fix is a swipe, and a swipe alone cannot be the answer: **both mobile browsers claim the left-edge drag for their own back gesture** and never forward those touches to the page at all (only an installed, `display: standalone` PWA gets the edge to itself), and an invisible gesture is no affordance for a first-time user, a screen reader, or a hand that can't drag. So the affordance is a **button, duplicated on purpose** — the header hamburger stays, and a floating one sits at `bottom-left` of the viewport: the edge the drawer arrives from, leaving the bottom-right slot for a primary action, offset by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` so it clears the home indicator, and at `z-20` (under the `z-30` backdrop and `z-40` drawer) so an open drawer simply covers it rather than needing a second state to keep in sync. The **swipe ships too, as a shortcut**: drag right from a 48px left band to open, drag the open drawer left to close, threshold-based rather than finger-following so a cancelled touch can't strand the drawer half-open, guarded by a slope test (a vertical scroll is not a drawer gesture), a 10px deadzone (at dx = 0 every gesture looks vertical), an ancestor check that yields to anything horizontally scrollable, and a media query that makes it inert at `lg`, where the rail is furniture. The general rule this sets — a control every page needs belongs within thumb reach, gestures may shortcut it and never replace it — is recorded in `docs/DESIGN.md` under Narrow screens. — *Built: the `nav` controller's `swipe*` actions and the layout's second toggle; `test/integration/mobile_layout_test.rb` pins the bottom-anchored button and its safe-area offset. A bottom tab bar is the thing to reach for if the nav ever needs more than one destination down there; today one button beats duplicating a 15-item rail.*
